Grading the Grader

An audit of ChatGPT-4o mini scoring 50 student essays ("Distance learning" prompt), compared against existing human holistic scores.

About This Study

AI tools are increasingly used to help with tasks that used to require a human, and grading student writing may be next. This project asks a simple question: if an AI model grades the same essays a human teacher already graded, does it score students fairly across different economic backgrounds, or does it score economically disadvantaged students differently?

To test this, 50 real student essays were pulled from a public dataset (25 from economically disadvantaged students, 25 from students who are not), all responding to the same writing prompt. Each essay already had a score from a trained human grader. Each essay was then given to ChatGPT-4o mini, using the same scoring rubric the human graders used, to see how its score compared.

How to use this page: the cards below summarize the overall results. Below that, each sticky note is one student essay — click any note to see the full essay side-by-side with the AI's score, its written explanation for that score, and which parts of its explanation touched on which grading criteria (highlighted in color). You can also filter the essays by economic status using the buttons below the summary.

The Writing Prompt

Some schools offer distance learning as an option for students to attend classes from home by way of online or video conferencing. Do you think students would benefit from being able to attend classes from home? Take a position on this issue. Support your response with reasons and examples.

View the full scoring rubric (used by both the human graders and the LLM)

Essays are scored from 1 (minimum) to 6 (maximum). The distance between each score is considered equal.

Score 6

Demonstrates clear and consistent mastery, with only a few minor errors. Effectively and insightfully develops a point of view, using clearly appropriate examples, reasons, and evidence. Well organized and clearly focused, with smooth progression of ideas. Skillful, varied use of language and sentence structure, with few errors in grammar, usage, and mechanics.

Score 5

Demonstrates reasonably consistent mastery, with occasional errors or lapses. Effectively develops a point of view, generally using appropriate examples, reasons, and evidence. Well organized and focused, with coherence and progression of ideas. Uses appropriate vocabulary and some variety in sentence structure, generally free of errors.

Score 4

Demonstrates adequate mastery, with some lapses in quality. Develops a point of view using adequate examples, reasons, and evidence. Generally organized and focused, with some coherence. May show inconsistent facility with language, generally appropriate vocabulary, some variety in sentence structure, and may have some errors in grammar, usage, and mechanics.

Score 3

Demonstrates developing mastery, marked by one or more weaknesses: inconsistent critical thinking or inadequate examples/evidence; limited organization or lapses in coherence; weak vocabulary, inappropriate word choice, or lack of sentence variety; an accumulation of grammar, usage, and mechanics errors.

Score 2

Demonstrates little mastery, flawed by one or more weaknesses: a vague or seriously limited point of view with weak critical thinking; poor organization or serious coherence problems; very limited vocabulary or incorrect word choice with frequent sentence-structure problems; errors so serious that meaning is somewhat obscured.

Score 1

Demonstrates very little or no mastery, severely flawed by one or more weaknesses: no viable point of view or little/no evidence; disorganized or unfocused, resulting in incoherence; fundamental vocabulary errors or severe sentence-structure flaws; pervasive errors that persistently interfere with meaning.
